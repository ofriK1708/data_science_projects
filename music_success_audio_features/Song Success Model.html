Speaker notes · 30-second opener

In this project I tested whether the Spotify audio features of a song predict commercial success. I defined success as popularity above roughly the 70th percentile, trained a Random Forest with deliberate handling of the class imbalance, and picked the decision threshold from a precision-recall curve. I reached 0.78 accuracy against a 0.69 majority baseline. But the most instructive part is what I would fix before taking it to production: I found several sources of data leakage, and the whole thing lives in one notebook. I'd be happy to walk through how I would turn it into a service.

The research pipeline

What the notebook does today, from raw CSV to evaluation.

Imbalance handled on purposeBalanced class weights plus a decision threshold chosen from the precision-recall curve, instead of trusting accuracy at 0.5.
An honest ablationEngineered features (intensity, feel-good, production balance) scored 0.764 against 0.780 for raw features, so they were dropped.
Importance that isn't biasedPermutation importance instead of the tree's built-in measure. Top features: energy, danceability, instrumentalness.
Speaker notes

Name the leakage before they find it: the "Today" genre is a recency bucket (median year 2020) and popularity favors recent songs; no time-based split; 97% of songs share an artist with another song; 43 duplicate songs; the threshold was tuned on the same data the result is reported on.

How I would serve it

A proposed design, not built yet. Dotted lines are the parts that do not exist today.

One artifactPreprocessing and model saved together as one pipeline, so serving uses exactly the training transforms.
Threshold as configThe decision threshold is a business parameter, versioned with the model rather than hard-coded.
Retrain on a schedulePopularity changes daily, so labels age. Retrain regularly and record the label date.
Speaker notes

OOP answer: refactor into a FeatureBuilder (stateless transforms), a SuccessLabeler (threshold policy) and a SuccessModel that wraps the pipeline and exposes save, load and predict(threshold).

Testing answer: unit tests for transforms and label edges (62 gives 0, 63 gives 1), data-contract tests, a regression test with a metric floor, and a smoke test that one prediction returns a probability between 0 and 1.

What I would fix first

The evaluation is optimistic in known ways. This is the order I would tackle them in.

  • Leakage
    Remove or rework the recency-bucket genre and compare metrics with and without it.
  • Splits
    Train up to 2018, test from 2019; group folds by artist.
  • Data
    Deduplicate on track and artist at ingest; key everything by a stable track ID.
  • Tuning
    Choose the threshold on validation data and report on a separate test set; compare against logistic regression and gradient boosting.
  • Reproducibility
    Pin dependencies, save the artifact, move data out of git.
